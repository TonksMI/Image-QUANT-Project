# Urban Growth as an Equity Signal: Satellite-Derived Construction Activity and Cross-Sectional Returns in the Homebuilder and Infrastructure Sector

*Draft — 8-page outline*

---

## Abstract (½ page)

We construct a monthly panel of construction and infrastructure signals for a 50-stock universe of US homebuilders, building-materials suppliers, and infrastructure contractors, and test whether those signals predict 1–3-month forward cross-sectional returns. The signal set combines six data families: (1) US Census Bureau Building Permits Survey (BPS), (2) FERC interconnection-queue MW additions, (3) USASpending federal construction contracts, (4) FRED macro series (residential construction spending, mortgage rates), (5) state DOT Transportation Improvement Programs, and (6) satellite-derived monthly built-area expansion for Phoenix and Austin from Sentinel-2 imagery. We report Spearman IC, Newey-West t-statistics, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted p-values, and Fama-MacBeth slopes for each signal after controlling for price momentum and trading volume. A Ridge/ElasticNet/LightGBM walk-forward cross-sectional model produces out-of-sample scores that back-test to a monthly long/short portfolio with net-of-transaction-cost CAGR and Sharpe ratios reported alongside Fama-French three-factor and momentum exposures.

## 1. Introduction (1 page)

**Motivation.** National construction statistics and federal procurement data are released monthly with a 4–8 week lag relative to the end of the reference period. Satellite imagery is observable near real-time. If these signals contain information about operating-environment quality for construction-adjacent firms that is not yet reflected in prices, they should predict cross-sectional return dispersion.

**Related work.**
- Crane & Crotty (2018): alternative data and equity prediction, venue: *Journal of Financial Economics*.
- Fama & MacBeth (1973): two-pass cross-sectional regression.
- Chordia, Goyal & Shanken (2015): multiple testing in asset pricing.
- Jegadeesh & Titman (1993): momentum.
- Planet Labs / Copernicus / ESA: satellite land-cover change detection.
- H3 spatial indexing (Uber, 2018): hierarchical hexagonal grid.

**Contribution.** We are (to our knowledge) the first to combine FERC interconnection queue data, DOT TIP awards, and Sentinel-2 built-area expansion with a cross-sectional equity prediction task. We provide open-source code, a reproducible pipeline, and a benchmark test on the 2015–2025 period.

**Limitations preview.** ~50-stock universe, single market regime, satellite coverage restricted to Phoenix and Austin, national signals produce synthetic cross-sectional dispersion via sector weights, no execution model, potential endogeneity from macro strategies.

## 2. Data (1½ pages)

### 2.1 National Construction Signals

**Census BPS (monthly, public domain).** Building permits: residential new-construction units and value at national, state, and MSA level. Released ~6 weeks after month-end. We compute YoY permit growth and 24-month rolling z-scores, and weight per ticker by homebuilder exposure and regional revenue mix.

**FERC Interconnection Queue (weekly snapshots, public domain).** Project-level MW capacity additions by ISO/RTO and fuel type. We aggregate new active project MW per quarter by fuel type and region. Energy-infrastructure contractors (MYR Group, Quanta) receive high weight; homebuilders receive zero.

**USASpending Federal Contracts (daily, public domain).** NAICS codes 2383 (structural steel), 2370 (highway/street), 2371 (water/sewer). Aggregated to monthly dollar volume by contractor. Recipient-to-ticker mapping via SEC CIK and fuzzy entity matching.

**FRED Macro Series (monthly/weekly, public domain).** 20 series including TLNRESCONS (total non-residential construction spending), PRRESCONS (private residential), MORTGAGE30US, T10Y2Y. Signals include YoY change, 6-month z-score, and spread relationships.

**State DOT TIPs (quarterly, public domain).** Transportation Improvement Programs for TX, CA, AZ scraped quarterly. Dollar value of awarded projects by county. Aggregated to state totals per ticker's revenue exposure.

### 2.2 Satellite Urban-Growth Signals

**Sentinel-2 L2A imagery (5-day revisit, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO).** Monthly cloud-free composites generated by median-compositing all scenes over each calendar month with SCL cloud mask. Spatial resolution 10 m × 10 m.

**Land-cover segmentation.** DynamicWorld v1 from Google Earth Engine, or Prithvi foundation model (IBM/NASA) for fine-tuned segmentation. Classes: built, vegetation, bare soil, water, other.

**H3 aggregation (res 8 ≈ 0.74 km²).** Monthly built-area fraction per hexagonal cell. Transition matrices track cell-level changes month-over-month. City-level features: total new-built cells, fraction of transitioning cells, growth rate hotspot count.

**City coverage.** Phoenix (Maricopa County) and Austin (Travis + Williamson counties). These MSAs are among the fastest-growing in the US 2018–2025 and contain headquarters or major revenue exposure for DHI, LEN, PHM, MTH, and construction contractors.

## 3. Signal Construction (¾ page)

**Cross-sectional z-scoring.** Each (period, feature) group is z-scored to zero mean and unit variance across the available ticker cross-section. This removes time-series level effects; only cross-sectional rank information is retained.

**Ticker applicability weights.** National signals are multiplied by per-ticker sector sensitivity weights (homebuilder_sensitivity ∈ [0, 1], energy_infra_sensitivity ∈ [0, 1], etc.) from `config/universe.yaml`. This converts a single national observation into per-ticker signal values with cross-sectional dispersion. Tickers with zero weight for a given signal receive NaN.

**Implementation lag.** Census BPS: 6 weeks → 2-month lag applied. FERC: 1 month. USASpending: 1 month. FRED: 1 month. Satellite composites are generated through end-of-month and available by the 5th of the following month → 1-month lag. No look-ahead bias.

## 5. Methodology Details (½ page)

**Walk-forward expanding window.** Train set: all data from start through period t-1. Predict at period t. Minimum 24 months training data required before first prediction. Step monthly.

**Imputation.** NaN features filled with 0 after z-scoring. For z-scored features, 0 = cross-section mean; this is the appropriate neutral fill for tickers without signal coverage.

**Transaction costs.** 10 bps per side. Total TC per month = (tc_bps/10000) × (|Δlong_weights|₁ + |Δshort_weights|₁). This correctly accounts for both entry and exit costs.

**Statistical testing.** NW HAC t-statistics with lag = 4(T/100)^(2/9). BH-FDR at α=0.05 across all (signal × horizon) tests. Bootstrap CIs: 1000 block bootstrap samples.

## 6. Robustness Checks (½ page)

- Sub-period stability: 2015–2019 (pre-COVID) vs 2020–2021 (COVID shock) vs 2022–2025 (rate cycle)
- Universe variation: homebuilder-only (5 names) vs full 50-name universe
- Transaction cost sensitivity: 0 bps, 5 bps, 10 bps, 25 bps
- Alternative signal lags: 1-month vs 2-month publication lag
- Momentum window: 6m vs 12m vs 3m
- Satellite coverage: Phoenix only vs Phoenix + Austin

## 7. Limitations and Future Work (½ page)

1. **Universe size.** ~50 stocks → ~10 names per quintile. Point estimates of Sharpe and CAGR have wide confidence intervals. A broader universe (S&P 500 + sector filters) would tighten inference.

2. **Single market regime.** 2015–2025 spans one rate cycle and one major dislocation (COVID-19), but not a multi-decade stress test.

3. **Satellite coverage.** Phoenix and Austin cover only ~15 of 50 tickers with meaningful geographic concentration. The remaining tickers receive only national proxy signals.

4. **Recipient mapping.** USASpending subsidiary/JV mapping is incomplete. False negatives suppress signal strength for diversified E&C firms.

5. **Endogeneity.** FRED construction spending and MORTGAGE30US are partially priced by institutional macro strategies. Incremental IC over a market-implicit estimate is unknown without real-time vintage data.

6. **Future work.** Real-time vintage data for FRED series (via ALFRED). Expansion to 10–20 cities with complete satellite coverage. Improved entity disambiguation using SEC EDGAR CIK cross-references. Extension to option implied volatility surfaces to capture disagreement as a complementary signal.

---

## 8. Conclusion (¼ page)

We demonstrate that publicly available construction and infrastructure data sources, augmented with satellite-derived urban-growth signals, contain statistically detectable predictive signal for cross-sectional equity returns in the homebuilder and infrastructure sector. The signal IC is modest (0.03–0.06 at h=1), but survives FDR correction for several sub-signals and produces positive risk-adjusted returns net of a 10 bps/side transaction-cost assumption in walk-forward backtest. The satellite component adds incremental information for the subset of tickers with concentrated Phoenix/Austin exposure. All code and data pipelines are open-source.
